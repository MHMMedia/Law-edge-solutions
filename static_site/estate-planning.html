The Estate Planning Buyer

Estate clients buy on trust, not urgency

A personal injury lead converts on speed. Yours converts on education. So we build a funnel that teaches first and sells second.

01: TEACH

Answer the real question

"Do I need a trust or just a will?" "What happens if Mom dies without one?" When your site answers the question they're actually Googling, you've earned the call before a competitor enters the chat.

02: REASSURE

Lower the stakes

People avoid estate planning because it forces them to think about dying. Plain-language pages, real timelines, and a clear "here's what the first meeting looks like" remove the dread that kills conversions.

03: CAPTURE

Catch the slow decision

This is a months-long decision. A downloadable checklist or a short guide gets the email today, so a few well-timed follow-ups bring them back when they're finally ready to sign.

Search & Paid, Done Right

Two very different buyers: the planner and the executor

Someone setting up a trust and someone scrambling to probate an estate search differently and decide differently. We run a strategy for each.

SEO for the planner

Rank for "Salt Lake City estate planning attorney," "revocable living trust Utah," and the long tail under them. Planners research for weeks, so durable organic visibility wins more of them than ads ever will.

Local Services Ads (LSAs) for the executor

Probate searches carry intent. Google Local Services Ads put your Google Screened badge at the very top and bill per lead, not per click. That fits the high-value, deliberate client who picks on trust.

Content that ranks and reassures

Local detail matters here. In Utah, a personal-property estate of $100,000 or less can skip formal probate with a small-estate affidavit 30 days after death, while informal probate runs roughly 6–12 months with a $375 district-court filing fee. Pages that get those facts right read as authoritative to a grieving family, and to Google.
